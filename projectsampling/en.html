Motivation letter

Why I want to make SAMPLING

The friction of a body of one’s own

I want to make SAMPLING because I want to open a debate about who has been selecting on our behalf.

I wonder what we still have to do in this transition towards democracy. How much of that construction of the body and of Spanishness under Franco remains alive today. Which gestures did we learn to recognise as ours, and who decided which bodies could represent us?

I ask from an Iberian Peninsula that is far more plural, sweaty, transporous, porous, wonderful and open than we were told. From affection for the bodies that taught me to dance and the desire to encounter other memories.

In this first look, I encounter the three Fs: Familia, Fútbol and Flamenco, references of a national identity that has been constructed and exported. Between 2015 and 2016 I began a dialogue between breaking and flamenco as dances of outward and return journeys. A transatlantic relationship of forms, gestures, bodies, rhythms and knowledge, shaped by colonialism and processes of decolonisation. Through breaking, my body disobeys what I learned to recognise as Spanish dance. Flamenco also transforms my breaking.

Over these years I have stripped flamenco and zapateado of compás and perfect repetition. I question the instruction « bailar en hombre » in Vicente Escudero’s decalogue. I put pressure on the angularity, verticality, precision and restraint I learned to associate with that body, alongside the gestures of bullfighting. From breaking I remove hypermasculinity to retain abstract gestures, inversions and changes in dynamics. What remains when I remove those prescriptions? Sampling lets me choose, cut and recompose. I also select, and I want to open that decision to debate.

Datamoshing and databending open a metaphorical question about the corruption of images, systems and data. Through these transformations I want to debate political corruption and the acts of selection and manipulation that construct identities, nationalisms and ways of recognising ourselves. What is left outside? Which history do we end up accepting as our own?

I want to bring these questions into rehearsal. Dance them, listen to them, project them, share them and generate debate during the process, so that SAMPLING can become more nourishing.

The work

The friction of a body of one’s own

Project

A voice made of other bodies

How do I develop a distinctive way of dancing through what I have learned from other people?

A living counter-archive

SAMPLING begins with the gestures, rhythms and images that have shaped my body: breaking, flamenco zapateado, football, family memory and audiovisual culture. They have reached me through crews, teams, friendships, schools and screens. By bringing them into relation, I seek a voice of my own that makes this shared memory perceptible. The body is a source of rhythm, a place of listening and the starting point for new movement.

Sampling means taking a sound fragment out of its context and transforming it through cutting, repetition or layering. I bring this operation into the body: a step, a breath, an impact or a posture can be selected, recorded, altered in duration and returned to rehearsal. I am interested in how a gesture’s identity changes when it is passed on, cut up or reinterpreted.

Learning by watching, making something one’s own by transforming it

My learning also comes through television, music videos, VHS, DVDs, forums and shared files. Copying, observing and repeating were ways of knowing movement before I could name it. A breaking circle, a football stand or a screen organises different ways of watching and belonging. The piece listens to these ways of learning and asks what remains in the body when its reference images have changed.

This is not a collection of styles presented as a display of skill. Selection, displacement and reinterpretation allow me to examine what has choreographed me and how I can reorganise it. Singularity emerges through decisions made with materials that have travelled through many bodies.

Current state

What we have / what we need

Moving from a research archive towards an autonomous stage work.

What already exists

A clear artistic question and a genealogy in Digital Bodies, Echo & Delay and Los lunares del Puma. There is an archive of physical, sonic and visual materials, experiments with cameras and projection, material involving zapateado, breaking, floorwork, football and stillness, and an initial hypothesis for the stage journey.

The archive includes approximately 35–40 minutes of physical and visual research material. This describes a working collection, not a continuous version or an established duration for SAMPLING. We also have a mobile research setup, audiovisual documentation, maps and writing, open-studio experiences and a history of laboratory and mediation work.

What I want to develop

Revisit that collection to decide what remains alive, what has lost its purpose and what can continue only through transformation. Generate new experiments, compose relationships and durations, and refine transitions between preparing the setup, the body-screen, play, zapateado and disappearance.

I need studio time for the body and technical time to integrate image, sound, light and costume; outside perspectives to question decisions; encounters with participants from the earliest stages; and production conditions for full runs, technical documentation and the premiere. I am seeking residencies, artistic support and co-production to sustain different parts of this journey.

Stage

What might happen on stage

A proposed journey to be tested during creation.

Enter / prepare / transform

The piece proposes to begin as the audience enters. Sound holds the room while I prepare cameras, cables, shoes, microphones, reflective surfaces and a ball. Placing, adjusting and testing acquire choreographic duration and attention. The conditions through which movement, sound and image are produced become visible.

A zoom of presence

I remain still while my recorded body dances across me. The present body gives volume to a dance occurring in another time. When I move, the surface receiving the image folds and shifts. Cutting, scale and delay explore when I recognise my dance and when it becomes unfamiliar.

Expanding the measure

A zapateado scene without projection concentrates attention on impacts and listening. Sampled fragments of atonal guitar construct a long pulse that breaks off. The body sustains, displaces or suspends its accents; rhythm can continue in the audience’s attention after the strike stops sounding. Duration and transitions will be decided in rehearsal.

Disappear / remain

Light, image, mylar and costume may merge the figure with the space’s surfaces. Presence moves from a recognisable body to a fragment, shadow or trace. I investigate what remains when the body seems to disappear and how that withdrawal relates to the ending. Final order and form remain open.

Research and process

Methodology

Working methodology

Try, record, listen, write, select and try again.

Collecting and analysing materials

The archive brings together physical actions, images, sounds, references and memories of learning. I perform materials again and compare them with recordings: what the body remembers, fills in and transforms. Selection also involves removal. Each fragment is examined through rhythm, weight, duration, provenance and its ability to relate to other fragments.

Spoken reflection, writing and video diary

Talking after rehearsal, writing, drawing maps and recording reflections are compositional tools. They help me articulate what an experiment shifts, recognise recurring interests and preserve doubts the image alone cannot explain. The video diary connects the experience of dancing with subsequent observation. Notes help determine the next session rather than illustrate a fixed method.

Technical hybridisation and gestural exploration

I work with supports, falls, inversions, travelling, zapateado, breathing and stillness. Entry practices activate readiness, play and listening before fixing a phrase. I isolate a gesture, repeat it and modify one condition: speed, direction, weight, interruption or surface. That shift can reveal relationships a familiar combination of steps would not produce.

Sonic and digital research

I record impacts, dragging and voice to listen to them as materials. Loops and delay return the body’s own trace in another time. I compare an action with and without capture, changing framing, scale or delay. I observe when the tool opens a possibility and when it adds a layer I can remove.

Designing the conditions of appearance

Space, light, surfaces and costume contribute to an action’s meaning. Preparing a camera or moving a surface can be part of the choreography. I document settings so an experiment can be repeated while keeping composition open. The criterion is to clarify what each relationship makes perceptible rather than accumulate effects.

A session and its return

I begin with a specific question, activate the body and prepare a focused experiment. I carry it out, record what is needed, review sound and image, and note a decision or a new question. Inviting other people to perform material allows me to observe it from outside. Shared sessions feed the same circuit: encounter, individual rehearsal and another encounter.

Laboratories

Three intersecting laboratories

The body produces a signal, receives it transformed and changes with it.

LAB A

Acoustic body

Supports, zapateado, breathing and dragging produce sound. Using contact microphones, I explore impacts, accents and pauses; I record and transform fragments to observe how they affect weight and the next action. Silence and interruption also alter what the body sustains. I seek an expanded rhythmic measure that remains active in listening when the foot stops marking it.

LAB B

Body-screen

I project a recording of my moving body onto my still body: dancing on top of myself. Skin, volume and costume folds distort the image. I vary framing, scale and time to ask what is projected onto the body and what the body projects through its presence. The performer observes, operates and is transformed by the setup.

LAB C

Dances that travel and return

I investigate the transatlantic circulation of bodies, gestures and rhythms associated with hip-hop and flamenco through their traces in my practice. Their histories are not interchangeable: I want to locate transmissions, transformations and erasures. This laboratory opens to others so that origins and ways of learning can enter into dialogue with composition.

Mediation

The friction of another body

If a voice of one’s own grows through learning from others, research needs to meet other bodies.

Encounters designed with each context

I propose a laboratory group developed with the host space and participants. It could take the form of weekly encounters during a rehearsal period or several intensive days. Pace, ages and conditions will be agreed with the context; the proposal does not assume an existing group. Previous Digital Bodies and Dansa Metropolitana experiences offer a starting point in shared practices of body, camera and installation.

Bring, transmit, transform

Each person can bring a gesture learned through dance, sport, screens or everyday life, describe its provenance and pass it on. We explore what changes when we repeat, listen to or record it, or let someone else interpret it. Roles of observing, acting and operating are exchanged. A phone or camera enables decisions about framing, image-making and what is left outside the frame.

What may change in the work

Encounters introduce readings that do not appear when working alone: what someone recognises in a gesture, which memory it triggers and what transmission changes. These observations return to rehearsal and affect selection, durations and relationships between scenes. I then share new experiments and decisions with the group for further exploration and transformation. This goes beyond a final consultation on a finished piece.

What it may open for participants

I want participants to recognise their references as creative material, experiment with body, sound and image and make decisions about their own proposals. Making the tools visible allows us to discuss how representation is constructed and try other ways of presenting ourselves. Exchange may develop listening, confidence and attention to other perspectives; these are aims to review with the group, not guaranteed outcomes.

Keeping traces of the dialogue

Notes, conversations and recordings agreed together will provide continuity. The group can propose questions and practices and choose the materials it wants to explore. An open-process event will share experiments and doubts with audiences in a format agreed with the host. The photographs show earlier laboratory and mediation experiences, not a SAMPLING activity that has already taken place.

Process

A process with permeable stages

Body, sound and image interact from the first experiments; the focus shifts as creation develops.

Revisit, transform and share

Organise Digital Bodies materials for their transition into SAMPLING. Perform them again, listen to recordings and decide what to retain, transform or discard. Begin the mediation dialogue and produce an initial selection of materials, questions and new experiments.

Develop the body and composition

Deepen zapateado, phrases, pauses, durations and transmission. Relate selected materials and explore the piece’s arc of attention. Work without projection too, to recognise what the body sustains on its own.

Deepen the relationship with technology

Develop the dialogue between body, camera, projection, delay, sound and light. Integrate costume and rehearse the relationship between performing and operating. Test transitions and the setup’s behaviour under stage conditions.

Integrate and refine

Compose the complete journey, remove layers and clarify durations. Run through the piece, compare perspectives and document decisions and technical needs for the premiere. Stages overlap and may require returns; they do not describe already secured residencies or schedules.

Production horizon

Spring 2028 at Antic Teatre in Barcelona is the preferred and most likely scenario, with the date still to be agreed. November 2027 remains an option if resources and funding arrive in time. Antic has included SAMPLING in its submitted Triennals proposal, pending a decision. Residencies, contributions and collaborators’ involvement will be agreed with each venue.

Setup

Body, image, sound and light

A research setup that participates in composition.

Image and surfaces

The proposed configuration uses four projectors fed by one central video source through a 1 × 4 HDMI splitter. The shared signal creates a synchronised frontal field across body, costume and surfaces. Mylar occupies the left side and extends towards the back. The camera connects capture, recording and live presence.

Sound and light

Ableton Live, Push 2, Zoom H6, contact and vocal microphones are among the research tools. Capture allows impact to be treated as sound and returned to movement. Light modifies projection legibility and restores presence to physical space; its design develops within rehearsal.

Working conditions

Integrating the setup requires controlled darkness, a suitable dance floor, stable projection positions and time for installation and testing. Earlier physical research can use simpler means. The plan is indicative and not to scale: lenses, brightness, distances, layout and resources will be adapted with each venue. It is not a final technical rider.

Costume

Costume as landscape

Recover, cut, recompose: another way of sampling.

A clothed body receiving the image

I will develop costume through upcycling, transforming existing garments. I will test tones, volumes and fabrics that support dancing, zapateado and projection. Relief and folds change the image as the body moves: the clothed body can become a landscape.

References combine knitwear, sportswear, footwear, mesh, transparency and reconstructed shirts. They suggest operations and possible silhouettes rather than final SAMPLING designs. Choices will be made through movement, lighting and camera experiments.

See and hear the process

Active memory

Documentation allows comparison between what happened in the studio and what appears in the image, revisiting decisions and sharing questions with those supporting the process. It brings together body and projection experiments, workshops, installation, writing and composition maps.
