01 / THE WORK

A living counter-archive

Sampling consists of taking a sound fragment, extracting it from its context and transforming it by cutting, repetition or layering to compose something new. In SAMPLING, Adrián Vega transfers this operation to the body: a step, a breath or a hit against the ground can become a sample that repeats, moves and transforms. The body occupies the center of this journey: it is the source of rhythm, a place of listening and the starting point of a new movement.

The sound impulse becomes weight, support, impulse and duration. A gesture produces a sound; that sound, repeated or altered, returns to the body and modifies its next action. In this round-trip circuit, the body samples and is sampled: it listens to what it has produced and returns it transformed.

This body that receives, transforms and returns is also the starting point to address the round-trip dances: gestures, rhythms and techniques that travel between territories and bodies, and return altered by each encounter. Flamenco zapateado, breaking, football, popular culture, family memory and digital images coexist as a living archive. Each gesture contains transmissions, appropriations and erasures. By cutting them, repeating them and recomposing them, the performer asks himself how to build a personal voice with movements shaped by many bodies.

The play begins as the audience enters. A rhythmic structure supports the room while the performer prepares cables, cameras, shoes, microphones, reflective surfaces and a soccer ball. The stage gradually becomes a recording and projection device. Each action creates an image, each image returns to the body and each return transforms the movement.

Four projections create a frontal field of multiplied images. The body receives them through costumes, skin, mylar, and stage surfaces. The performer operates the device and then enters its delays, visual traces and errors. Choreography, image, sound and light continually rewrite each other.

What returns in a dance after having traveled through other bodies and how can a solo become a personal voice within that shared history?

02 / STATUS OF THE WORK

From research material to the complete work

01

What already exists

  • A clear artistic and political premise, focused on the body as a living archive.
  • Approximately 35 to 40 minutes of physical and visual research material.
  • Choreographic material based on footwork, breaking, ground work, soccer and stillness.
  • A first dramaturgical arc and a defined connection with Digital Bodies.
  • Experiments with body camera, projection, screen costumes, mylar and live sound.
  • A mobile research device to develop image, sound and lighting.
  • Videos, images and a public sharing of the research process.
02

What I want to solve in Graner

  • Compose the complete route from the developed materials.
  • Refine the transitions between device preparation, body-screen and flamenco footwork.
  • Specify the duration of the scenes and test the end of the piece.
  • Integrate movement, sound, image, light and costumes in the same composition.
  • Contrast decisions with external views and with an encounter with the audience.
  • Perform complete passes and adjust the relationship between interpretation and technical operation.

04 / RESEARCH METHOD

Compose, observe, try again

The process alternates execution, recording, observation and recomposition. These three laboratories allow me to test movement, sound and image decisions and return to them during rehearsals.

LAB A

acoustic body

Footwork, flamenco foot percussion, breathing and dragging produce sound. I will investigate how its recording, repetition or transformation modifies listening and movement.

LAB B

Body-screen

In this laboratory I will investigate how and what can be projected onto the body, and what that body projects through its presence and its movement.

LAB C

Back and forth dances

In this laboratory I will investigate the transatlantic circulation of bodies, dances and rhythms around hip hop and flamenco. I will draw on these influences in my own practice to explore what is transmitted, what is transformed and what memories remain in the back and forth dances.

Prepare and transform

The piece proposes to begin during the audience enters. A sound time holds the room while I prepare cameras, cables, shoes and surfaces. These preparation actions are part of the scene: they allow us to observe how I arrange the tools with which I am going to produce and transform movement, sound and image.

I am interested in working on that transition between preparing the space and starting to dance. The body that organizes the device is also the body that activates it and is exposed to the audience’s gaze. I will investigate how the gestures of placing, adjusting and testing can acquire a choreographic duration and attention, making perceptible the conditions of appearance of images and sounds.

Project my digital body onto my static body

He body-screen It appeared when I projected my own videos onto my body to dance on top of myself. The scene is based on that situation: I remain still while a recording of my body in motion is projected on me. The present body gives volume to a dance that happens in another time. I call “presence zoom” this meeting between analog and digital body.

The projection encounters an irregular surface: the relief of the body, the folds and the shapes of the costumes cut out and deform the image. I will investigate what can be projected onto that body and what it itself projects with its posture, its presence and its way of maintaining stillness. When I start to move, the surface that receives the recording also changes: my volume folds and displaces my own image.

The record transforms the action into a fragment that I can cut, repeat or delay. By modifying its scale and duration, I will explore the friction between recorded time and present time. I am interested in observing when I recognize my dance, when it becomes strange and how the movement of the image can affect the body that receives it. These relationships will be refined during the investigation.

Expand the rhythm cycle

A long tap scene, without projections, will focus attention on the relationship between the body, impacts and listening. It will have its own time so that the accents, repetitions and variations of a phrase can be perceived, as well as the effort of sustaining it.

I will work from sampled fragments of atonal guitar to construct a long pulse that is interrupted. The zapateado will enter into relationship with that pulse: sometimes it will sustain its accents; Other times, it will displace a blow or leave space for silence. From these interruptions will arise a expanded rhythm cycle, in which the rhythm remains active when listening even when the body stops marking it.

What happens to rhythm when a body stops sustaining it for a while?

The transition from the body-screen to this scene will be decided during creation. Its duration and breaks will be tested in residence.

Disappear: gradations of invisibility

After the body exposed in the zapateado, I will investigate a gradual disappearance. The light, the image, the mylar and the costume can make the figure blend in with the surfaces of the space: sometimes it is recognized in its entirety; Others it is reduced to a fragment, a shadow or a trace of movement.

I am interested in testing different degrees of visibility and observing how the perception of a presence changes when it is no longer clearly distinguishable. What remains of the body when it seems to disappear? The duration of this retreat and its place in the finale will be decided during rehearsals in Graner.

07 / DEVICE UNDER RESEARCH

Body, image, sound and light in one composition

The planned configuration uses four projectors fed from a central video source through a 1 x 4 distributor. The shared signal creates a front field of synchronized image on the body and stage surfaces.

COMPUTER / LIVE CAMERA ↓ VIDEO PROCESSOR ↓ HDMI DISTRIBUTOR 1 x 4 ↓ FOUR PROJECTORS ↓ BODY + SCENIC SURFACES

Space

Black box, controlled darkness, front audience, dance floor and stable projection positions.

Image

Planned configuration of four projectors, 1 x 4 distributor, computer, camera and mylar. The availability and necessary resources will be agreed with the center.

Sound

Ableton Live, Push 2, Zoom H6, contact microphones, voice microphones and room sound equipment.

Light

Light controls the visibility of the image and returns all its presence to the physical space.

Adrián Vega works professionally as a lighting technician. He can preview, program and adapt the lighting language from within the creation process. The optics of the projectors, the brightness, the projection distances and the exact positions will be defined during the work in the room. The test configuration will conform to the resources agreed with Graner.

08 / COSTUMES

Costume as a landscape

The costumes will be developed through upcycling, recovering and transforming existing garments. Its tone and shape will be chosen to favor projection. The light and images will transform its appearance, turning the clothed body into a moving landscape. I will test how the fabrics respond to light and movement, also taking into account the freedom necessary to dance and tap.

These visual references guide the investigation of costumes: mixing codes and garments, transforming fabrics and recomposing fragments. I will test how these operations respond to movement and projection, to develop my own silhouette for SAMPLING.

09 / MEETINGS AND OPENING

Share what a body learns

I propose agreeing with Graner on an open studio with conversation or a gesture transmission laboratory, depending on the moment of creation and the center's interlocutors. The meeting would allow them to share the process and hear how their materials are read.

Each participant could contribute a learned gesture, transmit it and explore its repetition, registration and transformation. Sharing its origin would allow us to locate who transmitted it, in what context it was learned and what changes when another person executes it. The observations would return to the work of composition.
