SAMPLINGCUERPO PANTALLA

A body samples what history has already written into it.

SAMPLING investigates the construction of a cultural counter-archive through the body-screen. Inherited gestures, rhythms and images pass through a body that selects them, transforms them and puts them back into circulation. Physical action, its capture and its return through sound and image produce frictions that become part of the composition.

01

Synopsis

A body receives gestures that other bodies have danced, rhythms that have travelled and images that have taught ways of looking and moving. In SAMPLING, Adrián Vega works with these materials through his experience in breaking, flamenco footwork, floorwork, football and audiovisual creation.

The body functions as a screen: it shows, filters and transforms what passes through it. A step can become sound; its repetition returns to the performer and changes the movement. The camera crops a gesture, projection alters its scale, and delay allows an action to encounter its own image at another moment.

Through cutting, repetition, layering and emptying out, the piece investigates how to construct a cultural counter-archive: a composition that changes the relationships between inherited forms and opens up other readings of their histories. Mismatches, residues and returns emerge between physical presence and its digital transformation.

SAMPLING asks what remains when an image is removed or a compás (rhythmic cycle) is no longer marked, and how a voice of one's own can emerge among materials that come from many bodies.

02

Body-screen and cultural counter-archive

The body-screen asks how something appears: what the body shows, what it filters, what it conceals, and how its technique, its memory, image and sound intervene.

The cultural counter-archive asks what relationships we build with what appears: where it comes from, what narrative accompanies it, what had been left out, and how its meaning changes when it encounters other materials.

Adrián's body is the place where these two questions intersect, because it already has a specific history and also makes decisions about how to show it. The materials chosen condition what can appear; the way they are made to appear transforms how they are read.

The archive of SAMPLING is made up of learned techniques, music, family memory, popular culture and audiovisual images. The selection begins with situated experience: the points of support and shifts of breaking, the rhythmic work of flamenco footwork, dribbling a football, listening and the relationship with the camera. Each material brings learning, uses and associations that the composition can shift.

The counter-archive is constructed by changing those relationships. A recognisable form can appear fragmented, acquire a different accent, lose part of its image or encounter a movement from a different source. Decisions about what to preserve, what to interrupt and what to return matter, as do the histories that become perceptible along the way.

This research raises questions about flamenco as a sign of Spanishness, national imaginaries, the transmission of gestures and cultural images that are repeated until they become familiar. SAMPLING approaches these questions through the materials that have reached this body and the possibilities of transforming them on stage.

03

Friction between the analogue and the digital

The research works with the materiality of the body and the apparatus: weight, breath, impact, floor, cables, microphones, camera, light and reflective surfaces. A strike produces vibration; a surface reflects light; movement changes the conditions of listening and seeing.

Capture and processing introduce new decisions. Framing selects part of the action, recording preserves a duration, and playback allows that trace to be cut, repeated, amplified or shifted. A sound can continue after the gesture has ended. An image can return while the body is already doing something else.

Frictions arise between physical action, its capture and its digital return: changes of scale, repetitions, delays and losses. SAMPLING investigates how these transformations affect movement and the reading of the cultural materials that pass through the body.

The return also modifies the action. The performer listens to what he has just produced, sees a fragment of his movement and responds to that information. The composition develops between the body's decisions and the apparatus's possibilities and limits.

Digitisation thus participates in constructing the archive. What is recorded, from where and how it is reproduced affects what can be recognised and remembered. The apparatus is part of that selection and can be made visible through its manipulation on stage.

04

Sampling as a choreographic practice

Sampling consists of selecting a fragment and working on its relationships with other materials. In SAMPLING, the operations of cutting, repetition, displacement, layering and emptying out circulate between sound, movement and image.

A step, a breath, a strike against the floor or a football dribble can become material for composition. The performer changes its duration, its intensity, the point of support that produces it or the moment when it appears. When he performs it again, memory, attention, technique and the physical conditions of that moment come into play.

The research brings together three dimensions: the screen organises what is visible; the body articulates movement; sampling allows composition with fragments and temporalities. An operation can move from one medium to another: cutting the continuity of a gesture, repeating an image or removing a sound while the movement continues.

Appearance, withdrawal and compás

What happens to the compás when a body does not sustain it for a while?

This question opens a line of composition connected to stillness and emptying out. The experiments propose establishing a regularity, removing part of the information and observing what happens during that absence and on its return.

The audience's listening and gaze are part of the research: how long a structure remains recognisable, what is expected to return, and how perception changes when it returns differently. The process will explore relationships between visual accumulation, interruption and the reduction of material. Its organisation on stage will develop during rehearsals.

05

Research grounded in experience

SAMPLING draws on tools developed in Digital Bodies — Echo & Delay, at El Palacio in Gijón/Xixón, and enters into dialogue with the research of Los Lunares del Puma and Inaudit. The relationship between body, listening, image and space runs through these processes and contributes materials to this creation.

The work gathered includes approximately 35 to 40 minutes of physical and visual research material: footwork and floorwork, breaking, football, stillness, and experiments with the body-camera, projection, costumes as screens, mylar and live sound. It includes audiovisual records and a public showing of the process.

The next phase proposes reviewing, selecting and composing these materials through the relationship between body-screen and cultural counter-archive. The piece will develop towards a duration of approximately 60 minutes, exploring transitions, different forms of return and the perception of materials as they appear, transform or disappear.

Questions running through the process

  • What can a screen be when it is a body?
  • How does movement change when the body receives back the sound or image it has just produced?
  • What is preserved, what is lost and what cultural relationships change when a form passes through other bodies, territories and times?
  • What happens to the compás when a body does not sustain it for a while?
06

Three connected laboratories

The laboratories offer complementary tools for composition, public research and encounters with communities. Their practices run through the different phases of creation.

Acoustic body

The floor functions as a resonant surface and the body generates sound through feet, hands, knees, back, breath, falling and dragging. Flamenco footwork and breaking meet in the exploration of support, impact and the displacement of rhythm.

Listening to the sound produced, its amplification and its repetition make it possible to investigate how a sonic trace changes the next movement. The laboratory includes experiments in interrupting and withdrawing the compás.

Body and image

The camera, framing, projection, costumes, mylar and light make it possible to work with different forms of appearance. The body participates as performer, operator and projection surface.

Relationships between action and image are investigated: coincidence, changes of scale, delay, layering and disappearance. Manipulation of the apparatus can also become part of the composition, making the decisions that construct what is visible perceptible.

Bailes de ida y vuelta

Gestures, rhythms and techniques circulate between artistic languages and media. The research works with materials from flamenco, breaking, football, hip hop, family memory and audiovisual culture.

It explores these round trips: what happens when a fragment passes through other points of support, other durations or audiovisual reproduction and then returns to the body. Attention focuses on its transformations, what it preserves and the cultural associations that change along the way.

08

Stage apparatus

Body, sound, light and four projections

The planned configuration is a black box with a front-facing audience and four synchronised projections. The image is distributed across the body and stage surfaces, making it possible to investigate how the shape, movement and material of each surface affect what appears.

Sound connects impact and breath with their amplification and processing. Light modifies the relationship between the projected image and the performer's physical presence. The apparatus enables work on both the accumulation of information and its withdrawal.

The visual signal comes from a central video source, with camera input and processing, and is distributed through a 1 × 4 HDMI splitter. The four projections share that signal.

AreaPlanned configuration and requirements
SpaceBlack box, controlled darkness, dance floor, front-facing audience and stable projection positions.
ImageFour compatible projectors, computer, camera, video processing, 1 × 4 HDMI splitter and projection surfaces.
SoundAbleton Live, Push 2, Zoom H6, contact and vocal microphones, and the venue's sound system.
MaterialsCostumes as screens, mylar, cables and elements used in the bodily research.
LightLighting design integrated into the composition of image, movement and space.

Adrián Vega brings his professional experience as a lighting technician to the development of the apparatus. Lenses, brightness, projection distances and final positions will be specified during technical work in the venue.

09

Mediation and encounters with audiences

Mediation develops in relation to the creation and draws on the experience of the Digital Bodies laboratories. The encounters propose working with young people, students, artists or neighbourhood groups through gestures learned in the family, sport, dance and through screens.

Each participant can contribute material and explore what happens when it is repeated, shared, recorded or reframed. Decisions about what to show and how to show it make it possible to observe the relationship between bodily memory, image and perception.

The formats will be adapted to each context:

  • The body as archive. Recognise learned gestures and share the journeys each person associates with them.
  • Sampling a gesture. Repeat, shift and recompose a fragment of movement using bodily, sonic and audiovisual tools.
  • Body-camera. Explore framing, gaze, delay and projection; alternate between the positions of performer, recorder and observer.
  • Open studio. Share rehearsal materials and discuss what is recognised, what changes and what remains after an action.

The reference laboratory lasts three hours; the format, continuity and conditions of participation will be agreed with each organisation. Questions and observations emerging from these encounters may return to the composition process.

11

Production, connections and needs

SAMPLING is being developed in dialogue with production at Antic Teatre, in Barcelona. The research maintains a connection with El Palacio and the cultural context of Gijón/Xixón through the Digital Bodies process.

The next phase seeks spaces and collaborations that can provide continuity for rehearsals and advance the composition of the piece. Current needs are:

  • Connected creation residencies, with time for bodily, sonic and audiovisual work.
  • Access to a black box to test the full four-projector configuration.
  • Funding for rehearsals, collaborations and technical development.
  • Support in dramaturgy, sound composition and image throughout the process.
  • Spaces for mediation and public experiments connected to the research.
  • Support for production, tour preparation and distribution.

The planned premiere is November 2027. Residency periods, collaborations and presentation conditions will be agreed with production and the venues involved.

Adrián Vega

Choreographer, performer, educator and visual artist. His practice brings together breaking, hip hop, flamenco, contemporary dance, sound and moving image. His experience in audiovisual creation and lighting is part of his writing for the stage and his work with apparatuses.

His projects include We Are Just Okay, Los Lunares del Puma, Inaudit, with Adriano Galante, and Digital Bodies — Echo & Delay. His work connects bodily research, listening, audiovisual composition and the transmission of movement tools.
